Average Cost for Toilet Installation in Willingboro, NJ

The average cost for toilet installation depends on the toilet, the floor and what is under it. We explain what moves the price.

By the Willingboro 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated 2026. 6 minute read.

Call Now

The average cost for toilet installation is not one number, because two toilets that look alike can need very different work. A swap of one standard toilet in the same spot is the simple case. Moving a drain, fixing a rotted floor or fitting a wall-hung unit changes everything.

We do not post fixed prices here, since the real price depends on your house. This post explains what moves it and how to compare quotes.

Cost

What goes into the price

The toilet itselfBasic two-piece models cost far less than one-piece, comfort height or dual-flush designs.
Rough-in distanceThe measurement from wall to drain center must match the toilet. A mismatch can mean a different model.
Condition of the flangeA cracked or corroded flange needs repair before the new toilet will seal.
Subfloor conditionSoft or stained flooring around the base may need patching.
Shutoff valve and supply lineOld stop valves and rigid risers are often replaced during the swap.
Removal of the old toiletDisposal can be added if the old unit is heavy cast or you have no way to dispose of it.

Insurance rarely pays for a planned replacement. It may help if a slow leak rotted the floor, but the claim usually depends on whether the damage was sudden. Keep photos of the old flange and floor.

Typical job types and what changes the work.
Job typeWhat it usually involvesWhat can raise the cost
Same-spot swapRemove the old toilet, set a new wax or foam seal, connect the supply.A corroded flange or old shutoff valve.
Upgrade to comfort heightSame drain, taller bowl, and often a new seat.A rough-in that does not match the new model.

Wall-hung toiletIn-wall carrier and tank, finished wall.Framing, tile work and access to the wall cavity.

Rough-in is the number most buyers miss. Measure from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the floor bolts. Many toilets fit a 12 inch rough-in, but 10 and 14 inch layouts exist, and a toilet bought for the wrong one will not sit right against the wall.

The flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet bolts to. It should sit flush with or slightly above the finished floor. If new tile or vinyl raised the floor since the last install, the flange may now sit too low, and the seal can leak without any sign for weeks.

Install it yourself or hire it out

Reasonable for a handy owner

  • Replacing a toilet in the same spot
  • Replacing a seat or fill valve
  • Turning off water and moving the old unit
  • Comparing models by rough-in size

Better left to a plumber

  • A cracked flange or rotted floor
  • Moving the toilet or adding a bath
  • A wall-hung or in-wall tank system
  • Any leak that returns after a reseat

A toilet weighs a lot, so lifting it is the first hazard. A dropped bowl can crack tile or hurt a back. Turn off the supply at the wall or under the tank, flush to drain it, and sponge out what remains before moving the unit.

Get the room ready first

Clear the space

Move rugs, cabinets and anything that blocks a straight path to the door.

Know your shutoff

Find the valve behind the toilet and the main, and test that both turn.

Plan the old unit

Decide how the old toilet leaves the house and who carries it.

Protect the floor

Lay a drop cloth or old towels. Wax and old water stain finished flooring.

Willingboro, NJ: permits, contractors and who may do the work

The Willingboro Construction Official, Inspections and Code Enforcement office is at 1 Rev. Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Drive, phone 609-877-2200. It reviews permit applications and enforces the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code. Its page links a Homeowner's Guide to Permits and a document called Hiring Home Improvement Contractors, so read both before you sign anything.

That office does not list plumbing permit rules on its page, so permit rules vary and the safest move is to ask the Willingboro building department before work starts. A one-minute phone call can save you from tearing up a finished job.

New Jersey law at N.J.S.A. 45:14C-1 says plumbing shall be performed by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor, or their employees. Ask any plumber for the master plumber license and check it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers.

How to compare toilet installation quotes

  1. Ask what is included: removal, new wax or foam seal, new supply line and shutoff valve.
  2. Ask who supplies the toilet. A plumber supplied unit may cost more but comes with fewer fitting surprises.
  3. Ask what happens if the flange or floor is damaged once the old toilet is lifted.
  4. Ask whether the quote covers permit paperwork if the building department requires it.
  5. Compare the whole job, not the lowest line.

Two quotes that differ a lot usually differ in scope. One may include a new shutoff valve and supply line while the other assumes yours are fine. Lay them side by side and ask each plumber to explain the gap.

Related work often comes up at the same visit. If the fill valve runs, the flapper leaks or the tank rocks, our toilet repair service can fix those parts before they become a new install. If you are adding a bathroom, our plumbing installation work covers new supply and drain runs.

Water efficiency matters in the long run. Many newer toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush, while older units use more. A newer model can lower water use over time, but the saving is only one part of the decision.

FAQ

Questions from Willingboro homeowners

Is a more expensive toilet worth it?

Often the extra cost buys a better flush, a quieter fill and easier cleaning. A basic model that matches your rough-in works fine for many homes.

Does the toilet installation cost include removal of the old toilet?

It depends on the quote, so ask. Some plumbers include haul-away and others charge for it separately.

How long does a toilet installation take?

A same-spot swap often takes one to two hours. A damaged flange or floor adds time.

What if the new toilet rocks or leaks at the base?

A rocking toilet can mean an uneven floor or a flange set too low or high. We shim or repair the flange, then reset the seal.

Do I need a licensed plumber to install a toilet in Willingboro?

New Jersey law says plumbing is performed by a master plumber or authorized plumbing contractor. For what applies to owners doing their own work, ask the Willingboro building department at 609-877-2200.
