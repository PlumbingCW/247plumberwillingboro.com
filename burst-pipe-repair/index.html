Finding your shutoff valve

The main shutoff is usually where the water line enters the house, often near the water meter or the heater. Many homes also have a shutoff at the meter that belongs to the Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority. For a water emergency on the utility side, WMUA lists 609-877-3875. Walk your house now and tag the valve before you need it.

Frozen pipes and January in Willingboro

Willingboro sits in hardiness zone 7a, and January averages 32.8 degrees Fahrenheit. That puts night lows below freezing often enough to threaten pipes in unheated spaces, outside walls and garages. A frozen pipe can split without leaking until it thaws, so the flood starts when the temperature rises.

  • If a faucet gives only a trickle, open it and keep it open. A pipe may be frozen but not yet split.
  • Warm the area with a hair dryer or a heater placed safely away from anything flammable. Never use an open flame.
  • Shut the main if you see any dampness on the pipe.
  • Call us if the pipe is bulging, cracked or inside a wall.

To prevent a repeat, insulate exposed lines, seal drafts near pipes and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. Disconnect garden hoses before the first hard freeze.

Repairing copper, PEX and cast iron

A burst copper pipe is usually cut back to sound pipe and replaced with a new section, using soldered or press fittings. PEX can often be repaired with a crimp or expansion coupling, because the tubing is flexible and survives some freezing. Cast iron drain pipe cracks differently and is more likely to need a section replaced with modern pipe and couplings. We tell you which approach fits what we find.

A spot repair works when

  • The split is in one short section
  • The rest of the pipe is in good shape
  • Pipe is easy to reach
  • Nothing else has leaked recently

Larger repair is better when

  • Several pipes have failed
  • The pipe is corroded along its length
  • Walls hold a pinhole leak pattern
  • Water pressure damage is likely again

If several leaks appear in different spots, ask us whether repiping would cost less in the long run.

Why pipes burst in the first place

Freezing is the best known cause, but not the only one. Corrosion thins old pipe until a pressure surge splits it, and a loose fitting can blow apart under normal pressure. A burst at a joint often points to a failed fitting, while a long split along the pipe suggests ice or fatigue. Knowing which helps us decide whether one repair is enough.

Water damage and drying

We stop the leak and repair the pipe, and that is where our job ends. Drying walls, removing wet insulation and mold control are a separate trade, and we can tell you what to ask a restoration company. Acting within a day matters, so call that company as soon as the water is off.

FAQ

Questions Willingboro homeowners ask

Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Willingboro?

Yes. Our line runs 24/7 and we send a plumber for after-hours bursts, subject to crew availability. The price is confirmed before work starts.

Should I turn off the water heater?

If the main is off and water has stopped, turning off the heater breaker or gas control is a good precaution. Leave it off until we check that the tank is full.

Can a frozen pipe be fixed without replacing it?

Sometimes. If it has not split, thawing slowly may be all it needs. If it has split, the damaged section has to be cut out and replaced.

Who handles the line outside the house?

The Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority at 609-877-2900 can tell you where its responsibility ends. We cover water service line repair on the private side.

Will my insurance pay for the repair?

It depends on your policy and the cause. Sudden bursts are often treated differently from slow leaks, so ask your insurer before you decide.
