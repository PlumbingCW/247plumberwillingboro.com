Helium Testing Leak Detection for Willingboro, NJ Homes

Helium testing finds small hidden pipe leaks without opening walls, but most household leaks never need it. Here is how it works and when a simpler method wins.

By the Willingboro 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated 2026. 6 minute read.

Talk to a plumber
(609) 547-1417
Call Now

Helium testing leak detection pushes a harmless tracer gas into a pipe and uses a sensitive handheld sniffer to find the exact spot where the gas escapes. It can locate pinhole leaks that a listening device misses. It is also slower to set up, so we reach for it only when cheaper methods fail.

Most Willingboro leaks are found with a meter check, a pressure gauge or a look under the sink. Whether tracer gas is available on a given job depends on the line, the access and the crew, and we tell you up front if it is the wrong tool. Our leak detection visits always start with the simplest test that can answer the question.

Method

How tracer-gas testing works

A tracer gas is light and escapes through tiny openings far faster than water. We use either pure helium or a hydrogen mix, commonly about 5 percent hydrogen in nitrogen, which is sold as non-flammable at that strength. The sniffer reads parts per million at the surface, so the highest reading marks the leak.

  1. 1

    Isolate the line

    We shut valves and cap the section under test so the gas stays in one run of pipe.

  2. 2

    Empty the pipe

    Water blocks the gas, so we drain the section or blow it out with air first.

  3. 3

    Fill with tracer

    The gas goes in at a modest pressure that is safe for the pipe.

  4. 4

    Scan and mark

    We sweep the floor, wall or yard slowly, mark the peak reading and confirm it before anything is opened.

Readings are relative, so we first sweep a known dry area to set a baseline. Then we cover the suspect route in slow passes close to the surface. Wind outdoors and drafts indoors can scatter the gas, which is why we scan patiently and repeat any strong reading.

Pressurised lines and emptied lines

Supply lines that normally carry pressurised water are tested after they are drained, because the gas needs open space inside the pipe. Drain and sewer lines can be tested by plugging a cleanout and filling the section with the gas mix. Both jobs depend on reachable shutoffs or cleanouts, which is one reason availability varies by house.

Tracer gas earns its cost when a meter keeps turning, nothing is visible and listening has already come up empty. It is also useful under tile or a finished floor, where guessing wrong means breaking more than you need to.

Tracer gas has limits. The gas spreads through soil, concrete and wall cavities, so the strongest reading can drift from the true spot. Deep lines and thick floors weaken the signal. For that reason we confirm a result with a second check, such as a pressure drop or a listening pass, before anyone opens a floor.

Honest answer

When a simpler method is enough

A running toilet, a dripping valve or a stain below an exposed pipe do not need gas. Neither does a leak you can already hear. Pay for the cheapest test that gives a clear answer.

  • A meter test with every fixture off rules a supply leak in or out.
  • A toilet dye test finds a worn flapper in about 15 minutes.
  • A pressure gauge on a hose bib shows whether a closed system holds pressure.
  • Visible wet drywall usually points to a short list of pipes nearby.

FAQ

Questions from Willingboro homeowners

Is helium dangerous in my pipes?

No. Helium is inert, and the common hydrogen and nitrogen mix is sold as non-flammable. We still test in ventilated areas and follow the equipment instructions.

Will you have to cut into my walls?

Usually not before the leak is located. Scanning marks the spot first, so any opening is small and in the right place.

Can tracer gas find a leak under a slab?

It can help on some lines, but results depend on the soil, depth and surface. We will say if the odds are poor and suggest another approach.

How do I know whether I need it at all?

If a meter test and a visual check explain the leak, you do not. Call us if the meter moves and nothing visible explains it.

Can I use my WMUA meter to tell if I have a leak?

Yes, as a first check. Close every fixture, note the reading or the small leak indicator, wait 30 to 60 minutes and look again. Movement points to a leak, and WMUA can explain what its portal displays.
