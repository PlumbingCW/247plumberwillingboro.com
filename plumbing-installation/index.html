Homeowners looking for plumbing installation in Willingboro, NJ usually fall into two groups. Some are swapping a worn fixture for a new one, and others are adding supply and drain lines for a bathroom, laundry corner or kitchen remodel. We handle both with licensed plumbers, and our line is open 24/7 for booking.

This page explains what installation covers, what to have ready and what drives the price, so your project does not stall halfway through.

What we install

  • Kitchen and bathroom sinks, including faucets and sink drain plumbing
  • Toilets, bidet seats and supply stops
  • Tubs, showers and valve bodies
  • Washing machine hookups with a supply box and standpipe drain
  • New supply and drain lines for added or relocated fixtures
  • Outdoor hose bibbs and utility sinks

We do not do cabinetry, tile or drywall. We coordinate around your other trades, and we can rough in the plumbing before they close the walls.

Choosing fixtures before the plumber arrives

The fixture you pick decides what the plumber has to prepare. A wall-mounted faucet needs a different rough-in than a deck-mounted one, and a one-piece toilet may need a different flange height from a two-piece. Buy or at least choose the fixture first, then send us the model number so the right parts are on the truck.

New lines and PEX installation

When a fixture moves or a room is added, new pipe has to reach it. For supply lines we commonly use PEX, a flexible plastic tubing that bends around framing and uses fewer fittings than rigid pipe. Drain lines must slope and be vented, so their routing takes more planning than supply. Tell us early if the new fixture sits far from existing lines, because distance changes both time and cost.

If you are replacing most of a home's supply piping instead of adding to it, that is a larger job we cover under repiping.

Remodel rough-in and sink drain plumbing

Rough-in is the plumbing done while walls are open. Drain stubs, supply stubs and valve bodies are set, then pressure checked before the walls close. Doing this right once avoids opening finished drywall later.

Sink drain plumbing is a common stumbling block. A trap, a vent path and the right height at the wall must line up. A fixture that drains slowly on day one usually has a drain or vent problem, not a clog.

New construction plumbing

For a new addition or a rebuilt home, plumbing happens in stages. Underground drains go in first, then rough-in before walls close, then fixtures at the finish. Each stage is usually inspected before the next trade moves in. We can work with your builder's schedule, but we need plans or a clear scope to give a price.

Permits, licences and Willingboro homes

New Jersey law, N.J.S.A. 45:14C-1, says plumbing work must be done by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor, or their employees. Ask any contractor for the master plumber licence and check it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Willingboro building department. The Construction Official's office at 1 Rev. Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Drive, 609-877-2200, reviews permit applications and enforces the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code.

FAQ

Questions Willingboro homeowners ask

Do you install fixtures that I buy myself?

Yes, in most cases. Send us the model number so we can check that it suits your supply and drain, and we will tell you if a part is missing.

Can you move a sink or toilet to another wall?

Often yes, but it depends on where the drain and vent can go. We look at the floor or wall access and then quote the route.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work?

Permit rules vary, so ask the Willingboro building department at 609-877-2200 before work begins. We can tell you what we expect to be involved.

Do you offer 24/7 booking for installation?

Yes. Our line is open 24/7 and planned jobs get same-day appointments when available. Urgent leaks come first.

Why use a licensed plumber for new lines?

New Jersey requires plumbing to be done by a master plumber or authorized contractor. A licensed plumber also pressure tests the lines before the walls close.
