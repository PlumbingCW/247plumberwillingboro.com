When the main sewer line blocks, every drain in the house is at risk, and the lowest one overflows first. Homeowners in Willingboro, NJ can call us any hour for emergency sewer line cleaning. We answer 24/7 and book same-day visits when a crew is free.

While you wait, stop using all water, do not flush, and keep children and pets away from any floor drain or tub that has started to fill. Find your cleanout if you can, but leave the cap on until we arrive.

How this differs from drain cleaning and jetting

A drain cleaning visit usually targets one fixture, such as a sink or a tub. Hydro jetting is a high-pressure scouring method for walls coated with grease or roots. Sewer line cleaning is about the main line only, the large pipe between the house and the street, and it uses a larger machine and a heavier cable.

How we clear it from the cleanout

A cleanout is a capped pipe, often near the foundation or in the yard, that gives straight access to the main. We open it carefully, since a full line may release water and waste. A large cable with a cutting head then bores through the blockage, and we run water to confirm the line flows.

  1. Locate the cleanout and check what is draining.
  2. Open the cap slowly and let any pressure out.
  3. Run the large cable through the blockage and cut any roots.
  4. Flush with water and confirm every fixture drains.
  5. Offer a camera look if the clog was severe or has returned.

Where is the cleanout

Do not open a cleanout while water is standing in it, and do not pour a bucket down to see if it drains. Wear gloves, keep the cap and a rag nearby, and call us if you are unsure. A full line can push waste out of the opening faster than you can close it.

Cleanouts vary by house. Look for a capped pipe a few inches across, in the basement near the front wall, outside by the foundation, or in the yard in line with the street. If you cannot find one, tell us and we may need to pull a toilet to reach the line, which changes the price.

Scheduled cleaning for root-prone lines

Roots regrow when the opening in the pipe stays open. A root-prone line may need cutting every year or two, and we can suggest an interval after a camera run. If the same joint keeps letting roots in, a repair costs less over time than repeat cleanings. See sewer line repair.

FAQ

Questions Willingboro homeowners ask

Do you handle sewer line cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Willingboro?

Yes. Our line runs 24/7 and we send a plumber for after-hours main line backups when a crew is free. We confirm the price before work starts.

How do I know if it is the main line or just one drain?

If several fixtures are slow together, or the lowest drain gurgles, it is likely the main line. One slow sink is usually a local clog.

How often should I clean a root-prone line?

It depends on the line. A camera run shows how fast roots return, and we suggest an interval from that.

Can I clear the main line myself?

Rental cable machines can injure you and damage the pipe. We advise a plumber for a main line, and we do not recommend chemical cleaners.

Is the blockage in my pipe or the street main?

A cleanout check and a camera usually show it. If neighbors are backed up too, call the Authority at 609-877-4962.
