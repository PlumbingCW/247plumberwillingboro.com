Rittenhouse Park and its town homes

Rittenhouse Park is one of Willingboro's named sections. The Willingboro Public Library holds a Levitt and Sons brochure titled A New Community of Colonial Town Homes by Levitt and Sons for Rittenhouse Park. Library records also tie it to a tract that dates from about 1969 to 1973, later than the first sections.

The township's master plan request counts 10,994 single-family homes and 763 townhouses overall, but it does not say how many are in Rittenhouse. So we do not claim every home there is attached. Confirm that for your own address. Some Rittenhouse streets also break the rule that street names start with the park's first letter.

What attached homes change

In an attached home, plumbing sits close to a neighbor's. A leak from your side can reach their wall, and theirs can reach yours. Speed matters. If water is spreading, shut off your main valve, then tell your neighbor.

If your home is part of an association, read its rules before work. Responsibility for pipes, walls and common areas varies by community, and we cannot say what your documents require. Ask your association or property manager.

Utilities, permits and insurance

The Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority lists water emergencies at 609-877-3875 and sewer emergencies at 609-877-4962 on wmua.info. A problem in the street or at the meter belongs to the utility. A problem inside your house is ours.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Willingboro building department what applies. Homeowners insurance may cover sudden water damage but not every cause, so check your policy and photograph everything.

FAQ

Questions from Rittenhouse Park homeowners

Are all Rittenhouse Park homes town homes?

Levitt marketed the section as colonial town homes. We cannot say what every house is today, so confirm for your address.

What if water leaks into my neighbor's home?

Shut off your main valve, tell the neighbor and call us. Photograph the damage and check your insurance policy and any association rules.

Do I need my association's approval for plumbing work?

It depends on your community's rules. Ask your association or property manager before work starts.

Can you find where a leak starts?

Often yes. Leak detection traces moisture and sound to the source. A drip can travel along a pipe before it shows.
