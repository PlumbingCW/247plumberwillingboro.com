Main Sewer Line Clog Repair for Willingboro, NJ Homes

Main sewer line clog repair starts with a cable or jetter and a camera. Learn the signs, the options and how to tell whose pipe is blocked.

By the Willingboro 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated 2026. 7 minute read.

Talk to a plumber
(609) 547-1417
Call Now

Main sewer line clog repair usually begins with clearing the blockage using a cable or high-pressure water, then running a camera to see why it formed. If several fixtures back up at once, the clog is almost certainly in the main drain rather than one sink.

The right fix depends on what the camera shows. A grease plug needs different treatment from a root mass or a broken pipe.

Symptoms

Signs the main line is blocked

Several drains slow together

A basement floor drain gurgling when you run the washer points to the main.

Toilet bubbles or rises

Air pushed back through a toilet means the pipe is full downstream.

Sewage smell outdoors

Odor near the cleanout or yard can point to a leaking or blocked lateral.

Backups return in weeks

Roots and sagging pipe rebuild a clog quickly.

Water in the lowest fixture first

The basement shower or floor drain is where a backed-up main shows itself.

Roots, age and the cleanout

Roots enter through joints and cracks and grow into a mat. Grease and wipes build up on rough pipe walls. The Levitt homes in Willingboro began selling in June 1958, so many laterals are 65 or more years old, though we make no claim about the material in yours.

Most homes have a cleanout, a capped pipe that gives direct access to the lateral. It is often near the foundation, in the basement or in the yard close to the house. Finding it before a backup saves time, and so does keeping the cap clear of soil and shrubs.

Our hydro jetting service scrubs the pipe wall and often pairs with a camera pass. When the camera shows a collapse or a deep belly, that is a repair job rather than a cleaning job, and our sewer line repair team explains the options and gives the price first. Lining can suit a long run of cracked pipe when it is not collapsed, and digging suits a bad section that must be replaced.

Cabling and jetting work differently. A cable punches through a clog and drags debris, which suits paper or a soft plug. A jetter fires water at the pipe wall from a nozzle, so it removes grease and scale more completely. Neither can fix a crushed or offset pipe, and forcing a tool through one can make it worse.

Diagnosis

What a camera inspection shows

After the line is open, we feed a waterproof camera through the cleanout and watch the screen as it travels. It shows roots, grease, scale, offset joints, cracks and low spots where water sits. A locator at the surface can mark where the problem lies and how deep.

  • Ask for the footage or screenshots, since they support any repair quote.
  • Ask how far the camera ran and whether it reached the connection at the street.
  • Ask whether a belly, offset or break was found, and how far from the house.
  • Compare the repair option against how often the line has clogged.

Roots return if the entry point stays open. Jetting or a cutter head can keep a light root problem in check on a schedule, and a repair of the joint ends it. We tell you which route fits what the camera showed.

First steps

What to do during a backup

Do these first

Stop the water

Do not flush, run the washer or use the dishwasher.

Protect yourself

Keep children and pets out and wear gloves. Do not touch electrical items in standing water.

Move valuables

Lift boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor.

Photograph

Take pictures before you clean up, for insurance.

Call

Call us, and call the utility if neighbors are affected.

Prevention

Keeping the next clog away

  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste. Wipes of every kind belong in the trash.
  • Collect cooking grease in a can instead of pouring it down any drain.
  • Keep the cleanout cap clear and know where it is.
  • Watch for slow drains after storms and report repeat patterns early.
  • Schedule a camera look if the line clogs more than once a year.

A line that has been cleaned and inspected tends to stay clear longer, because the camera shows early signs such as a developing sag or a root hair at a joint. That makes planning easier than reacting to a midnight overflow.

FAQ

Questions from Willingboro homeowners

Can I clear a main line clog myself?

A plunger helps only with a fixture clog. A main line needs a machine, and a rented cable can damage old pipe if misused.

Will a camera inspection be needed?

It is wise after a main line clog that returns, because it shows roots, offsets and breaks so you fix the cause and not just the symptom.

Do roots always mean replacement?

No. Light roots may be cleared and managed, while a broken pipe usually needs repair. The camera decides.

Is a sewage backup a health risk?

Yes. Keep people and pets away, wear gloves and disinfect affected surfaces.

How do I tell if the clog is in my pipe or the township main?

Check the cleanout. If it holds water, the blockage is downstream and could be yours or the main. If neighbors also back up, call WMUA at 609-877-4962.
