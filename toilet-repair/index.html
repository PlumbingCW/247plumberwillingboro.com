A toilet that overflows or leaks at the base can ruin a floor and a ceiling below it. Our emergency toilet repair in Willingboro, NJ line answers 24/7, and we book same-day visits when a crew is free. Before we arrive, turn the small oval valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

Then lay towels around the base, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down if water is still rising. Do not keep flushing to see if it clears, because each flush adds more water to the bowl.

Overflowing or leaking right now

  1. 1

    Stop the water

    Close the supply valve behind the toilet. If it will not turn, shut the main water valve where the line enters the house.

  2. 2

    Stop the flushing

    Leave the handle alone until we say otherwise.

  3. 3

    Contain it

    Towels at the base, a bucket under any drip and a mop for the floor.

  4. 4

    Check other drains

    If a tub or sink also gurgles, the problem is the main line, not the toilet.

A toilet that backs up along with other fixtures points to a deeper blockage, and our drain cleaning crew handles that call too.

Clogged or broken: telling them apart

Probably a clog

  • Water rises slowly and drains slowly
  • A plunger moves the water
  • Other fixtures run normally
  • The toilet worked until recently

Probably a broken part

  • Water runs nonstop into the bowl
  • The handle feels loose or limp
  • The tank refills with a hiss
  • Water seeps from the base

When we arrive we shut the water, check the bowl and the tank, and look at the floor around the base before we touch a tool. That order matters, because a soft or stained floor changes the repair. We tell you what we found and what it costs before we begin.

Running toilet: flapper and fill valve

A toilet that hisses or refills on its own usually has a worn flapper or a tired fill valve. Water leaks from the tank into the bowl, and the fill valve opens to replace it. You will see the bill go up even though nothing looks wrong. A dye tablet in the tank, left for 15 minutes without flushing, shows color in the bowl if the flapper leaks.

The Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority offers a Smart Water Meter Portal, which can help you spot unusual use after a repair. A hidden toilet leak can waste a lot of water over a month.

Leaking base: wax ring and flange

Water at the base usually means the wax ring has failed, or the toilet has been rocking on a loose flange. We lift the toilet, inspect the flange for cracks, replace the ring and reset the toilet with new bolts. If the flange is broken or sits too low, we repair or shim it so the seal holds. A soft floor around the base needs a closer look before we reset anything.

Supply valve and other small parts

A supply valve that drips or will not close is its own repair, and we replace it so you have a working shutoff next time. Cracked tanks, bowls and tank-to-bowl bolts also leak and cannot always be patched. Porcelain with a crack is usually a replacement.

When to replace instead of repair

An older toilet with a cracked bowl, repeated clogs or a failing tank is often cheaper to replace than to keep fixing. Our plumbing installation page covers new toilets, including choosing a height and a flush type. Ask the Willingboro building department whether your project needs a permit, because permit rules vary. January averages near 33 degrees Fahrenheit, so a toilet in an unheated bathroom should have its supply line checked before a deep freeze.

Installing a new toilet

A new toilet is a short job when the flange is sound and the old fixture lifts out cleanly. We set the new bowl on a fresh wax ring, level it, connect the supply with a new line and check for leaks at the base and the tank. Bring a rough-in measurement from the wall to the bolts if you shop first, since 12 inches is common but not universal. Tell us if anyone in the house needs a taller seat, because comfort height bowls vary.

If the toilet fills very slowly, a partly closed valve is a common cause. For an emergency with the water service itself, the Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority water line is 609-877-3875.

FAQ

Questions Willingboro homeowners ask

Do you handle toilet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Willingboro?

Yes. Our emergency line is open 24/7 and we send a licensed plumber when a crew is free. We confirm the price before work starts.

Can I keep using a toilet that leaks at the base?

We advise against it. A leaking seal can soak the subfloor and the ceiling below, so shut the supply valve and call us.

Why does my toilet run on its own?

A worn flapper or a tired fill valve is the usual cause. Both are quick repairs in most cases.

Do you replace toilets too?

Yes. We install new toilets and can quote a replacement if the porcelain is cracked or repairs keep stacking up.
