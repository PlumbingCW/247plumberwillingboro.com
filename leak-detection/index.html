Water that is leaking now is an emergency, even when you cannot see it. Homeowners in Willingboro, NJ can call us any hour for emergency leak detection. We answer 24/7 and book same-day visits when a crew is free.

While you wait, close the main shutoff valve, photograph the stain or the wet area with a date, and move rugs and boxes away from it. If water is near outlets or a ceiling is bulging, keep clear of that room and switch off power to the area from the panel only if you can do it safely.

Meter still running or a high water bill

A water bill that jumps with no change in how many people live in the house is the most common first sign. The cleanest check is the meter itself. Shut every tap and appliance, note the dial or the low flow indicator, wait an hour without using water, and look again. Movement means water is escaping somewhere past the meter.

Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority lists a Smart Water Meter Portal on its site at wmua.info. Ask the Authority whether your account has access, because a usage graph that shows steady overnight flow points to a leak and not a habit. For a main break in the street or at the meter, the Authority's water emergency line is 609-877-3875.

Ceiling stains, wall damp and hidden drips

A brown ring that grows on a ceiling, paint that bubbles, or a wall that feels cool and damp usually means a supply or drain line is dripping above or inside it. Water can travel along a joist before it shows, so the stain is often not under the leak. We use a moisture meter and a thermal camera to map the wet area before anyone cuts drywall.

Under the house and in the crawlspace

Where a home has a crawlspace or an accessible underside, a dripping joint can soak insulation, rot wood and draw pests for months before anyone looks. Musty smell, sagging insulation and a floor that feels soft are the usual clues. Depending on the house, we check what access exists, since not every home in Willingboro is built the same way.

Buried and slab leaks

When supply pipes run under a concrete floor or in the ground, a leak does not show until it has been running a while. Signs include warm spots on the floor, the sound of water when everything is off, a drop in pressure and a bill that keeps climbing. We do not assume any particular home has a slab, and we confirm what is there before we say the leak is under one.

Acoustic listening gear picks up the hiss of a pressurized pipe through soil or concrete. A pressure test shows whether a line holds, and tracer gas can pinpoint a hard-to-hear leak. Once we know the spot, you choose between spot digging, a reroute or repiping.

Yard, meter box and irrigation leaks

A soggy patch in the lawn when it has not rained, or a meter box full of water, often means the service line between the street and the house is leaking. Because the Authority maintains a Lead Service Line Inventory and asks residents to help identify service line materials, a leak there is also a chance to learn what the line is made of. See our water service line repair page if the break is confirmed.

Irrigation systems leak at valves, heads and buried joints, and a stuck zone valve can run for days. A bill that climbs in summer while the lawn looks no wetter is the clue. We test the system by zone and tell you which zone to shut off.

Pool, spa and commercial leaks

Pool and spa leaks are one narrow case. If the plumbing between the equipment and the pool is losing water, we can pressure test the lines. If the shell or liner is the problem, that is a pool specialist's job, and we will say so.

We handle small commercial properties such as a shop with a running meter, but large buildings with multiple risers and specialized systems may need a firm with more equipment. Our commercial plumbing page explains where we fit.

FAQ

Questions Willingboro homeowners ask

Do you handle leak detection emergencies at night or on weekends in Willingboro?

Yes. Our line runs 24/7 and we send a plumber for after-hours leaks when a crew is free. We confirm the price before work starts, and we tell you how to shut off water while you wait.

How do I know if my water meter is showing a leak?

Turn everything off for an hour and read the meter before and after. If the reading changed, or the low flow indicator is spinning, water is escaping. Call us with what you saw so we can plan the visit.

Will you cut into my walls to find a leak?

Not until we have to. We map the damp area first with a moisture meter and thermal camera, and we explain what we found before suggesting a small opening.

Can you tell if the leak is on my side or the Authority's side?

Usually yes. If the leak is at or before the meter, the Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority is likely responsible, and its water emergency line is 609-877-3875. We find the spot and tell you which side it appears to be on.

Does the leak have to be repaired the same day?

Not always. Many leaks can be isolated with a valve until a repair is scheduled. We will tell you when waiting is safe and when it is not.
