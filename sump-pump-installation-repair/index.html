Signs your sump pump is failing

Humming but no pumping

The motor may be running while the impeller is jammed or the float is stuck.

Runs constantly

A stuck float, a failed check valve or a very high water table can keep it cycling.

Loud grinding or rattling

Worn bearings or debris in the impeller often make noise before failure.

Basin does not empty

Water that stays up after a storm means the pump is not keeping pace.

How to test your pump

  1. Make sure the pump is plugged in and the breaker is on.
  2. Pour a bucket of water slowly into the basin.
  3. Watch the float rise and the pump start.
  4. Confirm the water leaves through the discharge pipe and does not flow back.

If any step fails, call us. Test twice a year and before forecast storms.

Float switch, check valve and battery backup

The float switch tells the pump when to start. If it sticks against the wall of the basin, the pump may never run. The check valve on the discharge pipe keeps water from falling back into the pit, so a failed one makes the pump cycle again and again. A battery backup keeps a second pump running if the power fails, which often happens during storms.

Repair or install new

Repair is worth it when

  • The pump is fairly new
  • A switch or valve is the fault
  • The basin is sound
  • Discharge is clear

Install new when

  • The motor is worn out
  • The pump is undersized for the water
  • Rust or cracks affect the basin
  • You want a backup system

A new installation includes a basin, a pump, a check valve and a discharge line routed outside, away from the foundation and from the neighbor's property. Discharge into a sanitary sewer line is not something we would set up, because rules generally forbid it, so ask the Willingboro building department about requirements.

Sewage ejector pumps

A sewage ejector pump is a different machine. It lifts wastewater from a below-grade bathroom or laundry up to the main drain, and it uses a sealed basin. If an ejector alarm sounds or the toilet gurgles, call us. If the problem is a blocked main line, drain cleaning may be the first step.

Basement waterproofing and your foundation

Not every Willingboro home has a basement. Check your foundation type before planning anything, because crawl spaces and slabs have different options. Where there is a basement or crawl space, an interior drain along the footing can carry seepage to a sump basin, and a pump moves it outside. That is a plumbing solution. Cracks in the wall, exterior grading and gutters are separate issues we can point out but do not repair.

Rancocas Creek and Willingboro rain

Rancocas Creek drains Willingboro and forms its southwest boundary, and about 5.09 percent of the township's 8.14 square miles is water. Low ground near the creek can hold water longer after storms, so a working pump matters more there.

Levitt and Sons built many of the homes starting in 1958. Depending on the house, the foundation may differ, so ask us to look at yours rather than assume.

Keep the cord of a second pump separate from the first if you add a backup, and do not run extension cords through standing water. A pump on a dedicated outlet trips less often.

Storm prep checklist

  • Test the pump and listen for unusual noise
  • Clear debris from the basin and discharge pipe
  • Confirm the discharge line points away from the house
  • Test the battery backup and replace an old battery
  • Move stored items off the floor

FAQ

Questions Willingboro homeowners ask

Do you handle sump pump emergencies at night or on weekends in Willingboro?

Yes. Our line runs 24/7 and we send a plumber for after-hours pump failures, subject to crew availability. The price is confirmed before work starts.

How long does a sump pump last?

Many pumps last roughly 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they run. If yours is older, we advise planning a replacement before it fails.

Do I need a battery backup?

It is a good idea if your basement has flooded before or if storms often knock out power. A backup only helps if the battery is tested and replaced on schedule.

Should the discharge go into the sewer?

No. Sump water is groundwater and should be sent outside. Rules vary, so ask the Willingboro building department before you route it.

Do you waterproof basements?

We install sump systems and interior drains as the plumbing side. We do not do exterior excavation, wall sealing or foundation repair.
