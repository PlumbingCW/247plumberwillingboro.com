No hot water is an emergency in January and a leaking tank is an emergency in any month. Our line answers 24/7, and Willingboro callers get same-day appointments when a crew is free. This page covers emergency water heater repair for tank units, gas or electric.

If you see water pooling, turn off the cold supply valve above the heater and, for an electric unit, switch off its breaker. For a gas unit, set the control to pilot or off. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

Symptoms and what they usually mean

Lukewarm water

A failed lower element in an electric tank or a dirty burner and sediment in a gas tank often cause this.

Pilot keeps going out

A weak thermocouple, a draft or a failing gas valve is the usual suspect.

Rumbling or popping

Sediment on the tank bottom boils under the burner. Flushing can help if the tank is sound.

Water around the base

A loose fitting or a relief valve drip can be repaired. A leak from the tank itself cannot.

How we diagnose before we replace anything

We check supply and shutoff valves, the temperature and pressure relief valve, the anode rod, the thermocouple or elements, and the venting on gas units. A multimeter tests electric elements and thermostats in minutes. The goal is to fix a cheap part before anyone suggests a new tank.

  1. Confirm power or gas supply and reset safety controls.
  2. Test the heating components and thermostat settings.
  3. Check for sediment, corrosion and leaks at the tank seams.
  4. Give you the repair price and an honest age-based view on replacing instead.

When repair stops making sense

A tank that leaks from the shell is finished, and a unit well past 10 to 12 years with several failed parts is usually cheaper to replace than to nurse along. When that is the answer, we move straight to water heater replacement and explain the choices, including tankless units.

Repair makes sense when

  • The tank is not leaking from the shell
  • A single part has failed
  • The unit is within its normal life
  • Venting and gas supply check out

Replace instead when

  • Water leaks from the tank body
  • The anode rod is gone and the tank is rusted
  • Repairs keep stacking up
  • You want more capacity or efficiency

Local conditions that affect water heaters in Willingboro

Willingboro has a humid subtropical climate, with January averages near 33 degrees Fahrenheit, so incoming water is cold and a heater works hardest in winter. The Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority publishes an annual water quality report at wmua.info. We suggest reading it and testing your own tap, because mineral content drives sediment and shortens tank life.

Many homes in the Levitt-era sections have had a heater swapped at least once since 1958. If yours was installed by a previous owner, ask us to check the venting and the pan under it, since older installs often miss modern details.

FAQ

Questions Willingboro homeowners ask

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night or on weekends in Willingboro?

Yes. Our emergency line runs 24/7. We schedule same-day repairs when a crew is free and confirm the price before starting.

How long does a typical tank water heater last?

Most tank heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years depending on water quality and maintenance, so age is a big part of the repair or replace call.

Is a pilot light that keeps going out dangerous?

It can be. A pilot that will not stay lit may signal poor venting or a failing safety part. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Can you flush my tank instead of replacing it?

Often yes, if the tank is sound and the unit is not leaking. We check first and tell you if flushing is worth the time on a unit of that age.
