Sewage in the house and what it usually means

A single fixture that backs up is often a local clog. When the lowest drain in the house overflows first and several fixtures slow together, the problem is further down the line. Sometimes a cable clears it for a few weeks and the water returns, which suggests the pipe itself has a defect.

Backups that return after cleaning

A crack, offset or root opening keeps catching waste.

Gurgling and slow drains throughout

The main line is restricted somewhere downstream.

Wet or sunken patch in the yard

Wastewater may be escaping through a break and softening the soil.

Sewage smell outdoors

A break or open joint in the lateral can leak gas and water.

Your lateral or the municipal main

Responsibility splits at a line. The pipe from your house to the connection is your lateral. The main in the street belongs to the utility, and in Willingboro that is the Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority. Its sewer emergency line is 609-877-4962.

A camera usually shows whether the fault is on your side. If it is in the main, call the Authority. If it is on your lateral, we price the repair before work starts. Calling the Authority first is cheap and sometimes saves a repair bill.

Repair options: spot repair, lining or dig and replace

A short crack or a root opening at one joint can often be fixed with a spot repair, either a cut-out section or a small excavation. Where a long run is cracked but still holds its shape, a cured-in-place liner creates a new pipe inside the old one without a trench. A collapsed or deeply offset pipe usually needs digging.

Bellies, offsets and cast iron

A belly is a low spot where soil has shifted and waste settles. A camera shows standing water along the run. An offset is where two pipe sections no longer line up, which catches paper and lets roots in. Depending on the house, the original line may be cast iron, clay or plastic, and a camera shows which. Cast iron can scale and crack inside over time, so we look at the wall condition before lining it.

FAQ

Questions Willingboro homeowners ask

Do you handle sewer line repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Willingboro?

Yes. Our line runs 24/7 and we send a plumber for after-hours sewer backups when a crew is free. We clear the line first if we can, then quote the repair before digging.

Can the sewer pipe be fixed without digging up my yard?

Sometimes. Lining and small spot repairs avoid long trenches. A collapsed or heavily offset pipe usually needs excavation, and the camera shows which case you have.

Who pays if the break is in the street?

The utility maintains the municipal main. Call the Willingboro Municipal Utilities Authority sewer emergency line at 609-877-4962 if the fault is there. We can show you the camera footage.

Do I need a permit for sewer repair?

Permit rules vary, so ask the Willingboro building department at 609-877-2200. We will tell you what the job involves so you can ask the right question.

Can a cracked cast iron pipe be lined?

It depends on the wall condition. We look at the camera footage and tell you whether lining is a sound choice or whether replacement is safer.
